Use the [college financial aid calculator](/tools/compare-college-aid-offers/) to compare two offers for the same academic year. Federal Student Aid’s [guide to evaluating aid offers](https://studentaid.gov/articles/evaluating-financial-aid-offers/) explains which costs and funding sources to look for.

## Get the annual cost for each college

Start with the cost of attendance and account for expected school-related expenses that the estimate leaves out. Verify the housing and meal arrangement in each estimate and check whether the offer covers the full year or a shorter period. Ask the aid office about unclear amounts before relying on the comparison.

## Find the price after grants and scholarships

Subtract grants and scholarships from the annual cost. If one school costs $30,000 and offers $12,000 in grants and scholarships, the annual net price is $18,000. A school with a $36,000 cost and $19,000 in grants and scholarships has a lower net price of $17,000. Ask each school how much of the aid renews and what you need to do to keep it.

## Compare cash gaps and borrowing separately

Suppose you plan to accept $5,500 in loans at the first school and $4,000 at the second. The amount left to cover would be $12,500 and $13,000, respectively, before other sources of funding. The lower immediate gap comes with more borrowing. Treat work-study as expected earnings and check when you could actually receive the money. To explore what repayment could cost, use the [student loan repayment calculator](/calculator/) after comparing the two offers.
