Start with the payment you can fit into your budget, then compare how many payments each plan projects and what they add up to. Use the [LoanLens repayment calculator](/calculator/) to test the same loan and income assumptions under different plans.

## Compare the same loans

Use the same balances and interest rates for each scenario. Record the estimated monthly payment, repayment length, and total paid. If a plan bases payments on income, check how a raise or a change in household size could affect your estimate. Federal Student Aid's [guide to comparing repayment plans](https://studentaid.gov/articles/repayment-calculator/) explains what each estimate includes.

## Separate monthly affordability from total cost

Suppose one option projects $300 a month for 120 months and another projects $225 a month for 180 months. In this simplified example, the second payment is $75 lower per month and the total paid is $40,500, compared with $36,000 for the first option. These amounts illustrate how to compare results. Actual estimates can include payments that change over time.

## Check before you switch

If an estimate includes forgiveness, note how much and when. Verify eligibility and compare the estimates with [Federal Student Aid's repayment calculator](https://studentaid.gov/loan-simulator/) using your federal loan details. Ask your servicer about any account-specific deadline or change you need to make.
