## Income and household terms

**Adjusted gross income (AGI)** is a figure determined from income and adjustments on a tax return. A repayment plan can use it in the income calculation. When estimating a joint filing scenario, use each spouse's contribution once. Current pay and AGI can differ, especially after a job or household change.

**Discretionary income** is defined by the plan. In IBR the formula protects a portion of income tied to the poverty guideline. This is separate from the money you describe as optional in your personal budget. Other plans can define the income they use differently.

**Poverty guideline** here means the HHS table used for specific federal program calculations. Figures depend on household size and which geographic schedule applies. The contiguous states and DC share a schedule, while Alaska and Hawaii have separate ones.

**Dependent** in RAP refers to a qualifying dependent claimed on the tax return. **Family size** for IBR is determined under its rules. Check which people count under both definitions if you are comparing those plans.

## Loan and interest terms

**Principal** is the loan balance on which new interest is calculated. **Accrued interest** is interest that has accumulated. An account statement may show them separately. For an estimate, enter your balance from a current statement and check whether it includes unpaid interest.

**Capitalization** adds certain unpaid interest to principal, so it can affect future interest accrual. The event and amount depend on the applicable rules and account history. Ask about capitalization after any plan change or other event that could trigger it.

**Direct Loan** refers to the federal Direct Loan program. Its loan types include Direct Subsidized and Unsubsidized loans and Direct PLUS loans. With a Parent PLUS loan, the parent is the borrower. Graduate PLUS is borrowed for the graduate or professional student's own education.

**Direct Consolidation** pays off certain existing loans with a new federal consolidation loan. It can affect available plans, the rate and repayment term, and payment histories. **Private refinancing** replaces loans with a private loan whose terms are set in a new contract. Moving federal balances into that contract can remove access to federal programs.

## Repayment plan terms

**Income-driven repayment (IDR)** describes plans organized around an income calculation and applicable household rules. The payment can be updated when the borrower recertifies. Programs have distinct eligibility and forgiveness rules.

**RAP** means Repayment Assistance Plan. Its payment rule uses AGI bands, dependent adjustments and a minimum installment. When on-time payment conditions are met, certain unpaid new interest can be waived and principal may be credited.

**IBR** means Income-Based Repayment, an income-driven plan with different borrower classifications. The classification can depend on the status of older federal loan balances and the dates of newer loans. Its bill reflects discretionary income and a cap tied to a Standard payment.

**Legacy Standard** is the fixed-payment option for eligible pre-change debt. Legacy Direct Consolidation Standard may use a term determined by debt. **Tiered Standard** is the newer fixed schedule whose max term varies with the amount of Direct Loans at entry to repayment.

## Time and forgiveness terms

**Entry to repayment** is when the loan starts its repayment schedule. It can follow a grace period or other event. Find the original entry date before using it to check a tier or estimate how many fixed installments are left.

**Recertification** updates documentation used to calculate the income-driven bill. Your repayment account shows the timing. Periodic changes in income and household circumstances can change scheduled payments. Some electronic authorization settings may affect how the servicer receives that information.

**PSLF** means Public Service Loan Forgiveness. The program checks loan type, qualifying payment months and employer criteria. A **certified count** records months that have met the relevant program checks to date, and two loans can have different counts.

**Forbearance** and **deferment** are different statuses that can temporarily affect payment obligations. Interest and qualifying payment credit depend on the status, loan and applicable rules. Check both the reason and dates before assuming that a postponed payment changes your progress.

**Forgiveness** cancels an eligible remaining balance after program requirements are met. **Discharge** is also used for particular statutory cancellation paths, such as disability. Tax treatment can depend on the reason and tax year.

## Read a statement with those terms in mind

Review which amounts are contractual, which dates a servicer recorded and which values your worksheet estimates. Start with the principal, accrued interest, next bill and plan name. If you are tracking a plan change, add the application date and the first planned installment. For a forgiveness goal, keep the certified count by loan. If you share a scenario with someone, omit account numbers and identify estimates as estimates so the receiver can check the result.

## Records to keep

Keep a plain-language record of the names and types printed for each loan. Include the date you checked it and the source you used. If the information changes, retain both versions and note which one came first. This makes a call with a servicer or reviewer easier because you can explain the exact point that changed.

Next, write down any separate principal, accrued interest and next-bill amounts. Include these details in your own copy of the example and check both against the next statement. If a figure comes from a forecast, mark it as an assumption. Finally, identify the term or rule you need the servicer to explain. You can use that item as the next question if the answer still depends on someone else's records.

## Terms that look similar on a statement

An account can show an amount currently due and a total amount owed. The amount currently due is the installment to pay by the given date. The total amount owed may include principal and accrued interest across several loan groups. An amount quoted to pay off the loan on a specific date can differ from both figures because interest may continue to accrue until the payment posts. When asking about a bill, specify which figure you mean and which loan it belongs to. Keep a dated copy of the statement and compare the same fields at the next billing cycle.
