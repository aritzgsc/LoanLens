## Who this applies to

Use this guide if an estimate shows a balance remaining at the end of an income-driven plan or at a potential PSLF date. People whose loans are fully repaid before forgiveness do not have a projected discharged balance in that scenario.

## What to check

### Identify the type and date of discharge

| Outcome | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| End-of-term IDR discharge | The amount and effective date stated by the servicer. |
| Public Service Loan Forgiveness | The approval and date recorded for the qualifying loans. |
| Balance repaid | The closing statement showing that no balance remains. |

The IRS Taxpayer Advocate describes when amounts canceled under income-driven repayment generally become taxable cancellation-of-debt income. Public Service Loan Forgiveness is generally excluded from federal income under the service-based rules. Check the program named in your discharge notice before applying either rule. If your estimate spans many years, tax law could change before the discharge takes effect.

### Read the estimate as a balance

The projected forgiveness figure is the estimated amount of loan debt that remains when the plan reaches discharge. It is not the estimated tax owed. Total payments in LoanLens are payments to repay the loans under the plan. Those totals exclude any tax that may arise from a discharge. An option can show lower loan payments and still require a separate estimate of the tax effect.

For two eligible plans, compare the estimated discharge year, how much debt each leaves outstanding and the payment totals. Keep your assumptions about income and loans constant between runs. If your actual household income or filing status could differ in that year, record separate scenarios and avoid treating a single future tax rate as a fact.

### Check the federal and state treatment independently

Ask which tax year the cancellation is reported in and retain the discharge notice. If you receive an information return, compare its amount and date with the notice. If they do not agree, contact the issuer and keep the records used to resolve the difference. Check federal instructions for any exclusion your circumstances may allow. The IRS describes insolvency and other exceptions with their own requirements and reporting rules.

State income tax treatment can diverge from the federal rule. Check the revenue agency for the state relevant to the year of discharge. If the projected forgiveness would change your repayment choice, compare the plan results first, then separately estimate tax under the rules you can support today. Update that comparison if the expected discharge date or tax treatment changes.

### Example: forgiveness amount is not the tax bill

Suppose a future, officially approved **IDR discharge** cancels **$20,000**. That is a potential amount of cancellation-of-debt income, **not a $20,000 tax bill**. Any federal income tax, if applicable, would be calculated under the taxpayer's rules and circumstances for the actual discharge year. An otherwise similar **PSLF discharge** is generally excluded from federal income. State treatment can differ.

The IRS Taxpayer Advocate explains that cancellation under an income-driven plan in **2026 or later** is generally taxable and discusses exceptions such as insolvency. Do not treat any current tax bracket as a certainty for a projected discharge decades in the future. [Read the IRS guidance](https://www.taxpayeradvocate.irs.gov/news/tax-tips/what-to-know-about-student-loan-forgiveness-and-your-taxes/2026/03/).

## Common mistakes

- Treating the full discharged balance as the dollar amount of tax.
- Applying the general IDR tax rule to a documented PSLF discharge.
- Assuming a future state tax rule follows the current federal rule.
