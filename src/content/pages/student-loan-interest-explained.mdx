When you see a student loan interest rate of 6%, that is an **annual** rate, not a charge of 6% each month. Your balance, the number of days between payments and your loan's rules all affect the actual interest charged.

Understanding the difference between **principal**, **interest** and **total paid** makes repayment comparisons far more useful.

## Three terms that do most of the work

**Principal** is the amount of borrowed money still outstanding before interest. **Interest** is the borrowing cost that accrues under your loan's terms. **Annual percentage rate or stated interest rate** describes that cost per year, though fees and the exact presentation of rates can differ by product.

Your loan statement may show principal and accrued unpaid interest separately. A payment can cover accrued interest and then reduce principal, depending on the loan's servicing rules and allocation.

## Worked example: estimating one day of interest

Federal Student Aid explains that student loan interest generally uses a **daily interest formula** based on the principal balance, the annual interest rate and the number of days in the year.

Imagine a hypothetical loan with:

- Outstanding principal: **$10,000**
- Annual interest rate: **6%**
- A 365-day year for this illustration

The approximate calculation is:

**$10,000 × 0.06 ÷ 365 = $1.64 per day.**

If the principal stayed unchanged for 30 days, that would be roughly **$49.32** in interest over the period. The actual amount can differ because the principal changes as payments are applied, the period may be longer or shorter, and the servicer uses its applicable calculation rules.

This is an illustration, **not a payment quote** and not a claim about the amount a specific loan will accrue.

## Why a smaller monthly payment may cost more

A lower monthly payment can make a budget manageable, but if payments are spread over more months, the projected total cost can be higher. Some repayment programs may also involve changing payments or forgiveness rules.

Look at all four figures in the [repayment comparison calculator](/calculator/):

1. **Starting monthly payment:** what the estimate begins with.
2. **Time to payoff or modeled forgiveness:** how long the projection continues.
3. **Total projected paid:** the sum of payments across the modeled period.
4. **Assumptions and warnings:** why a projection might not apply to your exact loan.

Do not choose a plan only because its first payment looks lowest.

## What does unpaid interest mean?

Interest can accumulate even when you make no payment. Whether and when unpaid interest is added to principal (sometimes called capitalization), and whether any subsidy applies, depends on the loan type and rules. Do not assume all student loans accrue interest identically.

Federal Student Aid explains the difference between [subsidized and unsubsidized loans](https://studentaid.gov/articles/subsidized-vs-unsubsidized-loans/) and provides an [interest rates and fees overview](https://studentaid.gov/understand-aid/types/loans/interest-rates).

## Why LoanLens and your statement can differ

LoanLens uses a simplified **monthly** projection to make plan scenarios comparable. Federal student loan interest is generally calculated daily. Other differences can include payment dates, balance changes, unpaid accrued interest, fees, adjustments and repayment eligibility.

For an account-specific figure, use your actual servicer statement and the [official Federal Student Aid Repayment Calculator](https://studentaid.gov/articles/repayment-calculator/).
