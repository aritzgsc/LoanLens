Repayment becomes easier to understand when you separate three questions: **What do I owe? When is it due? What options can I actually use?** You do not need to answer them all at once.

This checklist is for U.S. federal student loans. Private lenders may use different processes and rules.

## 1. Identify every loan

Sign in directly at [StudentAid.gov](https://studentaid.gov/) and review the loans associated with your account. Keep a list of each loan's:

- Current balance and the separately displayed accrued interest, if available
- Loan type (for example, Direct Subsidized or Direct Unsubsidized)
- Interest rate and loan servicer
- Repayment plan and next due date, if listed
- Existing repayment or qualifying-payment history where applicable

If your servicer's account disagrees with what you expect, ask the servicer to explain it. Do not paste login credentials or full account numbers into an online planning tool.

## 2. Check the first bill and your budget

Look for your actual payment due date and amount in the servicer's communications. Build a simple monthly budget around essential expenses and the payment you have been quoted. Consider how a payment fits alongside rent, food, transport and any emergency savings you need.

An estimated payment is **not** a replacement for the payment displayed in your official account. Late or missed payments can have consequences, so contact the servicer promptly if the amount is difficult to manage.

## 3. Compare options with the same inputs

Two plans can have different starting payments, repayment lengths and total projected costs. Use your *same loan balances and rates* each time to make a fair comparison.

The [LoanLens student loan calculator](/calculator/) lets you test scenarios and see assumptions. The [Federal Student Aid Repayment Calculator](https://studentaid.gov/articles/repayment-calculator/) is the official starting point for checking available plans and eligibility with your account data.

Ask these questions about each option:

- Is my **loan type and borrowing history** eligible?
- Is the amount **fixed** or can it change after income recertification?
- How much would I pay over the modeled repayment period?
- Does the result assume qualifying forgiveness or future income growth?
- Is there a deadline or account action I need to complete?

## 4. Keep confirmations and dates in one place

Maintain a private record of your chosen plan, servicer messages, payment confirmations and any application status. If you are pursuing forgiveness, keep the relevant employer certification and official qualifying-payment counts.

LoanLens offers a [repayment deadlines helper](/deadlines/) and [PSLF progress tracker](/tools/pslf-payment-tracker/), but these depend on the figures you enter and cannot verify a notice, an employer or a payment history.

## 5. Know when to ask for help

Contact your loan servicer if you:

- Cannot afford the current payment
- See an unexpected balance or interest amount
- Have a deadline in a notice that differs from a general website
- Have consolidated loans or have Parent PLUS loan history
- Have a forgiveness count that seems incomplete

Do not rely on a generic calculator for legal, tax or eligibility decisions about your own account.

## Your first-payment checklist

- **Check:** I can find my loans and servicer in official records.
- **Check:** I know my actual next bill amount and due date.
- **Check:** I have checked the interest rate and type of each loan.
- **Check:** I have compared official repayment options.
- **Check:** I understand the difference between a modeled estimate and my actual bill.
- **Check:** I have saved confirmation of any account change.

For more official guidance, see [Federal Student Aid: How To Prepare for Student Loan Payments](https://studentaid.gov/articles/prepare-for-payments/) and the [repayment plans overview](https://studentaid.gov/manage-loans/repayment/plans).
