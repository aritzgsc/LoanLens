LoanLens answers common **U.S. student loan and college financial aid** questions using educational explanations and models. The FAQ is about what this website actually does; it does not provide personal borrowing advice.

## How can I estimate my monthly student loan payment?

Open the [student loan calculator](/calculator/) and enter a current loan balance, annual interest rate and loan type. Income and household information may be required for some modeled repayment options. Compare the resulting monthly estimate with the total projected cost and the displayed assumptions.

Your actual bill may differ. For account-specific choices and eligibility, use the [official Federal Student Aid Repayment Calculator](https://studentaid.gov/articles/repayment-calculator/) and your servicer.

## Is the lowest monthly payment the cheapest repayment option?

Not necessarily. A smaller monthly payment can extend the repayment period and result in a higher amount paid overall. Other repayment options can change with income, loan type and program rules. Compare **starting payment, repayment length, total projected paid and assumptions** rather than choosing only by the first dollar figure.

Read [How to compare repayment plans](/guides/compare-student-loan-repayment/) for a worked example.

## Why does the estimate differ from my loan servicer’s statement?

LoanLens models interest using monthly approximations. Federal loan servicing commonly uses actual daily interest calculations, dates, balance changes and loan-specific rules. Your statement can also include unpaid accrued interest or other adjustments the calculator cannot see.

Read [How student loan interest works](/guides/student-loan-interest-explained/) and compare with your account statement.

## Can I compare two college financial aid letters?

Yes. The [college aid comparison tool](/tools/compare-college-aid-offers/) takes each school's **annual cost of attendance**, grants, scholarships and the amount you plan to borrow. It shows net price and a remaining funding gap.

**A loan does not reduce the school's net price:** it is a way of financing that cost. Work-study is generally earned through work and should not be counted as an automatic tuition discount. Read [How to read a college aid offer](/guides/understand-college-aid-types/) for more context.

## Can LoanLens tell me if I qualify for PSLF?

No. The [PSLF tracker](/tools/pslf-payment-tracker/) projects possible remaining qualifying payment periods **from a count you supply**. It cannot confirm your employment, past payment history, eligible loans or forgiveness.

Check your actual record with [StudentAid.gov](https://studentaid.gov/) and use the [PSLF Help Tool](https://studentaid.gov/pslf/) for official actions.

## Can LoanLens tell me which deadline applies to my account?

The [repayment deadline helper](/deadlines/) can organize dates and show illustrative deadlines from the notice information you enter. It cannot read your servicer messages or determine a binding personal deadline. **Use the exact date and instructions in your official notice** when they differ.

## Does LoanLens require a login or collect my loan account information?

No LoanLens account is required. Calculations use numbers you type into the webpage. Do not enter private identifiers. Shared scenario links may contain the values you entered and should be handled cautiously. Read the [privacy policy](/privacy/) for the details, including hosting and potential advertising.

## Is LoanLens a lender or the U.S. Department of Education?

No. LoanLens is an independent educational website, not a loan servicer, bank, lender or government agency. Its [open-source repository](https://github.com/aritzgsc/LoanLens) documents its logic and tests, and the [Sources page](/sources/) links to official references.

## Where can I report an error?

Use the [Contact page](/contact/) to reach the project's public GitHub issue tracker. Describe the page and use made-up example values to reproduce the problem; do not include personal or financial account records.
