## Which plans can I compare?

The calculator compares RAP, the applicable IBR version and legacy Standard for supported older Direct Loan portfolios. For eligible portfolios that include a Direct Loan from the new-law cutoff onward it compares RAP and Tiered Standard. Federal loan eligibility depends on your record, including dates, loan types and prior consolidations. Parent PLUS and uncertain or mixed cases use separate guidance in the tools.

IBR and RAP both look at income, but they calculate it differently. IBR uses a protected amount tied to a poverty guideline and a cap linked to the ten-year Standard calculation. RAP applies an income band rate to AGI, subtracts a dependent amount and checks a payment floor. If a bill from the servicer differs, compare the AGI, balances, filing status, dependent count and timing used.

## What happens if I was in SAVE?

Read your loan servicer's notice. A first notice provides 90 days and a later final notice provides 30 days. An account may be moved to a fixed plan if you miss the final deadline. The notice contains the most relevant date. PAYE and ICR use the separate July 2028 transition. If you are looking at a headline about the transition, check which plan and date it discusses before taking action.

If you expect payment credit for a forbearance month, check the account and federal guidance for that status individually. Prior credit toward income-driven discharge is not the same as prior PSLF credit. If an earlier month still needs confirmation, keep it separate in your worksheet.

## Can I use RAP for Public Service Loan Forgiveness?

RAP is a qualifying plan under the PSLF repayment-plan test. You also need eligible loans and qualifying employment during qualifying months. The account may show different counts for different loans. The calculator's PSLF comparison assumes qualifying employment continues and uses a single count per scenario. For loans whose histories differ, estimate each separately.

The estimated PSLF savings compare one plan with and without the discharge. The number represents scheduled payments that would be avoided in that model. It does not predict tax, income or legal changes. Tiered Standard does not qualify under the PSLF plan test. The ten-year legacy Standard plan can qualify, though a new ten-year schedule can leave no remaining balance when you reach the payment count.

## How do I check a Parent PLUS consolidation?

Look up the date the consolidation loan was disbursed and the underlying debts. Federal Student Aid describes an ICR-to-IBR route for eligible earlier consolidations, including a payment under ICR. Ask which date applies to your account if you were already enrolled or an application is pending. Confirm that the payment was posted under the plan and to the particular loan. The parent is the borrower for this purpose.

Parent borrowers who also took loans for their own education should check if separate loan groups can use separate arrangements. One payment amount can understate the overall bill if the other group is on a different schedule. Get the full picture in writing before changing a loan through consolidation.

## Why can my balance rise under an income-driven plan?

If a scheduled payment fails to cover new interest, the unpaid amount may remain as accrued interest unless a plan benefit applies. RAP conditions its unpaid interest waiver and potential principal match on full, on-time payment. IBR can leave outstanding interest and has a limited subsidy for qualifying subsidized loans. Some events may capitalize accrued interest. The estimate uses a simple monthly model and flags assumptions that depend on actual account history.

A loan can also show a temporarily different balance if a payment was credited on a different day, if the billing cycle spans more days, or if prior unpaid interest was already present. Compare two statements over the same period before drawing conclusions from a single snapshot.

## Will sharing an estimate disclose my information?

The calculator runs on your device. When you choose to copy a scenario link, information you entered travels in the link fragment. Anyone with the full link can decode the input values. Share a link only with someone you want to see those figures. For a planning discussion, using rounded values or the example borrower can communicate the trade-offs without sending exact figures. Print the results instead if that fits the discussion.

## What should I do with the lowest-cost badge?

Use it to find the smallest modeled out-of-pocket payments under the current assumptions. Then look at the first bill, the timing of repayment and any balance that may be forgiven. A different income forecast, household or qualifying payment count can change the rankings. For a major change, confirm eligibility, taxes and the actual installment using your servicer, Federal Student Aid and an appropriate adviser.

## Records to keep

Keep a plain-language record of the calculator inputs you can match to current statements. Include the date you checked it and the source you used. If the information changes, retain both versions and note which one came first. This makes a call with a servicer or reviewer easier because you can explain the exact point that changed.

Next, write down the payment, end date and amount potentially forgiven for an eligible option. Include these details in your own copy of the example and check both against the next statement. If a figure comes from a forecast, mark it as an assumption. Finally, identify one scenario assumption worth changing before you rely on the estimate. You can use that item as the next question if the answer still depends on someone else's records.

## What if I only know the total balance?

Start with your account’s list of loans. Enter each federal loan separately when rates or types differ. A combined balance may conceal a newer disbursement or a Parent PLUS consolidation that changes eligibility. If you have details for only part of the balance, run a case for those loans and label it partial. Add the others once you can verify their types and rates. Use a separate calculation for any loan that has a different certified PSLF count. A portfolio total can be useful for budgeting, but it is the individual loan history that can determine which options are available.
