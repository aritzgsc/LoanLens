## Who this applies to

This applies if your student Direct Loans are eligible for the Repayment Assistance Plan. Check the loan type and disbursement dates first. Parent PLUS loans and consolidation loans that include Parent PLUS need their own eligibility check.

## What to check

### Find the numbers used for your bill

| Input | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| Adjusted gross income (AGI) | The federal tax return used to certify your income. |
| Claimed dependents | The dependents claimed on the applicable return. |
| Eligible loan balances | The loans included in the repayment calculation, including qualifying spouse debt where applicable. |

Start with the [RAP payment by income table](/rap-payment-by-income/) for a single borrower without eligible spouse debt. Its income bands show which rate the plan applies to the full AGI. Moving up a bracket changes the rate for that entire income amount. This differs from applying the higher rate only to the dollars above the bracket threshold.

### Calculate the starting payment

After selecting the bracket, divide the annual result into monthly payments. The lowest bracket has a separate annual amount in the rule. Then subtract {formatUSD(rules.rapDependent.value, 'whole')} per claimed dependent. If qualifying joint debt is split between spouses, the borrower’s share affects the amount. The minimum bill is {formatUSD(rules.rapMinimum.value, 'whole')} per month. The [married RAP tool](/tools/married-rap-impact/) compares filing inputs and displays the payment to expect for one borrower.

If your latest return is no longer representative of your income, check the official application instructions for acceptable documentation. When comparing plans, use the same starting income and debt assumptions so differences come from the plan rather than the scenario setup.

### Separate the bill from the change in balance

A RAP bill can be smaller than the interest that accrues that month. The plan provides an unpaid-interest benefit when its payment conditions are met. RAP also provides a principal benefit that depends on how much of your payment reduced the amount borrowed. Neither benefit reduces the required monthly bill you enter in your budget.

Use the starting payment to compare your immediate budget, then use the projected payments and ending balance for the long-term comparison. Changes in reported income or claimed dependents can change future payments after recertification. A projection that uses a constant household can therefore differ from your real payment history. If you have several loans, ask your servicer how it applies the payment and principal benefit across them before treating a loan-by-loan projection as exact.

### Check the exact income brackets at their boundaries

Using a borrower with **no claimed dependents** and no spouse-loan allocation, the RAP formula illustrates why entering the exact AGI matters:

| Sample AGI | RAP band | Base monthly payment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $60,000 | 5% of full AGI | $250.00 |
| $60,001 | 6% of full AGI | about $300.01 |
| $70,000 | 6% of full AGI | $350.00 |

At $60,000 with one claimed dependent, the illustrative starting amount becomes **$200.00** after the $50 dependent reduction. This is a sample payment formula, not an eligibility determination or a servicer quote. Check [Federal Student Aid's current RAP explanation](https://studentaid.gov/articles/faqs-idr-plan/).

## Common mistakes

- Entering gross salary when the calculation asks for adjusted gross income.
- Applying the higher rate to only the income above a band threshold.
- Subtracting the principal benefit from the monthly bill.
