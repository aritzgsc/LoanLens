## Who this applies to

This guide is for Direct Loan borrowers who see Tiered Standard in their federal account, including borrowers affected by new Direct borrowing on or after {rules.newLoanDate.value}. If your account instead shows legacy Standard, check its separate rules, especially for a Direct Consolidation Loan.

## Key facts and decisions

### Match the term to debt when you entered repayment

<table>
  <caption>Tiered Standard terms</caption>
  <thead><tr><th scope="col">Direct Loan amount at repayment entry</th><th scope="col">Repayment term</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>{rules.tierTerms.value.map(([upper,months],index) => {
    const lower = index ? rules.tierTerms.value[index - 1][0] : null;
    return <tr><th scope="row">{lower === null ? 'Below ' + formatUSD(upper ?? 0, 'whole') : upper === null ? formatUSD(lower, 'whole') + ' or more' : formatUSD(lower, 'whole') + ' to under ' + formatUSD(upper, 'whole')}</th><td>{months / annualMonths} years</td></tr>;
  })}</tbody>
</table>

Use the amount the servicer recorded when the loans entered repayment. Crossing a threshold today because you made payments does not necessarily change the original term. At a bracket boundary, use the tier that begins at that amount. Confirm the balance used to determine the term if you borrowed again, consolidated, or had multiple repayment start dates.

### Compare the bill against the remaining schedule

The fixed plan sets a monthly amount using the balance, rate and scheduled term. The rule uses a minimum payment of {formatUSD(rules.fixedMinimum.value, 'whole')}, with a provision for a remaining balance below that minimum. If you are already making payments, ask for the number of installments remaining and the maturity date. LoanLens’s fixed schedule is a fresh projection. Entering the remaining balance alone does not recover the original monthly bill.

A longer term spreads payments out, which can make the current bill easier to cover. It can also increase interest because the balance stays outstanding longer. To compare the cost of two terms, keep the starting balance and rates the same, then compare the scheduled total. If you want to refinance or consolidate to change terms, check eligibility and benefits before relying on the payment difference.

### Check forgiveness and plan-switch consequences

A fixed bill does not change with income the way an income-driven bill can. If your budget needs a different bill after an income loss, check whether RAP is available before assuming the fixed payment can be recalculated. Direct Loan type and disbursement history matter.

If your goal is PSLF, verify that your proposed plan and loan meet the qualifying repayment rules. Do not assume that a plan called Standard qualifies just because a different version does. If you need to change plans, obtain the latest eligibility and repayment history from your account before interpreting the calculator’s projected term.

Request a schedule that separates interest from principal if you want to test an extra payment. An extra payment can change the payoff date without changing the original term used to select the tier.

## Common mistakes

- Selecting the term using a reduced current balance in place of the amount at entry.
- Comparing monthly payments without checking total interest over the term.
- Assuming every fixed schedule counts as a PSLF-qualifying schedule.
