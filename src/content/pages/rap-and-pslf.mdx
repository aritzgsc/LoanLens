## Who this applies to

This applies to borrowers with eligible Direct Loans who work for a qualifying public service employer and want to compare RAP with another available repayment plan. It also applies if you are moving from SAVE and have more than one certified payment count.

## What to check

### Check the three conditions in your federal account

| Item | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Loan | Match the loan identifier and type to the payment count shown. |
| Employment | Confirm the employer and employment dates through the PSLF Help Tool. |
| Repayment | Check that the plan and payment period qualify for that particular loan. |

The target is {rules.pslfMonths.value} qualifying monthly payments. Employment periods can be certified as you go. Use the [PSLF payment tracker](/tools/pslf-payment-tracker/) for an estimate based on the count for one loan. If two loans have different counts, repeat the estimate and keep the dates separate.

### Read the timeline alongside the balance

A favorable monthly payment can still pay off a small balance before PSLF. Compare how much principal is left when you reach the qualifying count. If the balance reaches zero first, there is no remaining balance to forgive. If a result shows possible forgiveness, compare the payments needed to reach that date and the amount expected to remain.

RAP is also an income-driven plan with its own, separate discharge period. A month credited toward an income-driven plan is not automatically a certified PSLF payment. An employment certification, a posted payment and a qualifying payment count are separate items in the federal record.

### Handle gaps and multiple loans individually

For a gap during SAVE, identify the month and the loan status listed in your account. Federal Student Aid describes limited buyback eligibility for certain past periods. Buying time back must lead to forgiveness under the program. Check the federal instructions for the period and loan type before adding any month to a planning scenario.

If you consolidate or change plans during public service, write down the counts before the change and compare them with the counts posted afterward. Keep a copy of the submitted employment forms and the decision notices. If you see a missing period, match your employer’s dates and the servicing record before asking for a correction.

When your count reaches the requirement and the other conditions are met, use the PSLF form to request forgiveness. The date in a projection is useful for planning the next eligibility check. Use the official account and any servicing notice for the actual application and payment status.

## Common mistakes

- Applying the highest loan count to every loan in the portfolio.
- Treating a predicted forgiveness date as an approved discharge.
- Assuming every fixed plan or every forbearance qualifies for PSLF.
