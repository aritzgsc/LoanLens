## Who this applies to

This applies to parents who borrowed Parent PLUS or have a Direct Consolidation Loan that repaid Parent PLUS. A student’s Graduate PLUS loan is a different loan type. The parent should check the loan history in the parent’s own federal account.

## What to check

### Identify the loan and its disbursement date

| Account record | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Parent PLUS still listed separately | Check fixed options for the current loan. |
| Direct Consolidation Loan that repaid Parent PLUS | Check the disbursement date and any qualifying ICR payments. |
| Later Direct Loan or consolidation | Verify whether that borrowing changed the options for older loans. |

Federal Student Aid describes a transition for Parent PLUS debt consolidated before {rules.parentConsolidationBefore.value}. Check the date the consolidation loan was disbursed. An application date, first billing date or payoff date for an old loan can differ from the disbursement date.

### Verify the recorded ICR payment

For the applicable transition pathway, confirm at least {rules.parentIcrPayments.value} payment under ICR for the consolidation loan, within the applicable period. Get the payment date and the plan in effect when it posted. Use the [Parent PLUS checker](/tools/parent-plus-consolidation-checker/) to identify what to ask. If you have no qualifying payment recorded, ask the servicer about the deadline to enroll, the first available due date and the date the payment must be credited. A general transition date does not tell you how long account processing will take.

Federal Student Aid’s eligibility guidance also accounts for newer loans. If you received a new Direct Loan or consolidated again on or after {rules.newLoanDate.value}, check the effect on all affected Parent PLUS debt. New consolidation can change which plans the portfolio can use. Do not rely on an earlier eligibility determination after a new disbursement.

### Compare fixed options separately

If you cannot take the transition route, ask which fixed plan applies and get the monthly bill, balance, and remaining term. A projection for ordinary student Direct Loans cannot tell you whether a Parent PLUS record qualifies for IBR. Parents pursuing PSLF should check the parent borrower’s qualifying employer, payment count, loan type and repayment plan. The student’s employer or tax return does not substitute for the parent’s. If the dates in your records disagree, request the actual consolidation and repayment history before making a selection.

## Worked decision example: records to collect

**Case A — no consolidation:** a Parent PLUS loan is not directly eligible for RAP or IBR. Ask the servicer about available fixed plans and whether a consolidation pathway exists for your specific loan.

**Case B — consolidation before July 1, 2026:** check the **disbursement date of the Direct Consolidation Loan** and whether it has **at least one recorded payment under ICR** within the applicable transition period. An application approval is not proof that the required payment posted. The IBR exception is conditional.

**Case C — new consolidation on or after July 1, 2026:** do **not** assume an old Parent PLUS ICR-to-IBR pathway remains available. Verify current eligibility directly with your servicer and [Federal Student Aid](https://studentaid.gov/articles/save/).

These are decision questions, not a confirmed eligibility ruling or advice to consolidate. The [Parent PLUS tool](/tools/parent-plus-consolidation-checker/) helps you collect the relevant records.

## Common mistakes

- Using the application date instead of the consolidation disbursement date.
- Assuming time in ICR proves that a payment posted under ICR.
- Interpreting a student-loan example as a Parent PLUS eligibility decision.
