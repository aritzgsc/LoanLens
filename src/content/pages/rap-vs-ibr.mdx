## Who this applies to

Start here if your federal student loans were issued before the new Direct borrowing cutoff and you may have access to both income-driven plans. If you received new Direct Loans on or after {rules.newLoanDate.value}, check current eligibility before comparing IBR.

## What to check

### Compare the rules that can change your choice

| Feature | RAP | IBR |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Income used | A rate applies to full adjusted gross income. | Uses discretionary income after a poverty allowance. |
| Household | Uses the dependents claimed on the applicable tax return. | Uses family size and the regional guideline. |
| Payment lower bound | A minimum applies. | A sufficiently low calculated amount can produce a zero payment. |
| Longer-term balance | Interest and principal benefits can apply after a qualifying payment. | Interest treatment depends on loan type and repayment history. |

Find whether your IBR account is classified under the older or newer borrower rules. That label changes the rate and length of the plan. Check the cap shown for your IBR account or ask what amount the servicer uses. Starting from today’s balance alone can produce a different cap from an actual loan record.

### Compare at the same income and on the same date

Run both plans with the same loans, starting income, location and reported household. First compare monthly payments, then compare total payments for the full projection. Repeat the calculation for a plausible higher and lower income if either could change the decision. A higher first bill can still reduce the total paid when it pays down principal quickly.

For IBR, use the poverty guideline region attached to the calculation. Alaska and Hawaii have separate guidelines. A larger family size lowers the income counted under IBR’s poverty allowance. RAP uses claimed dependents as a monthly reduction instead. The definitions are different enough that the same household can produce different inputs for the two plans.

### Review the payment history before switching

Confirm credit already accepted under the destination plan. An estimate based on a payment count is only as accurate as the count you enter. If your account has a mix of loan types, separate recorded credits by loan before assuming they all share a discharge date. For public service, use the certified PSLF count and the loan’s eligibility to compare potential payments through discharge.

Compare how unpaid interest affects the outstanding principal when switching plans. The applicable triggers and the amount of unpaid interest already in your account can change the longer-term schedule. Check whether a calculator’s estimated forgiveness is a balance amount rather than projected tax. If that balance would drive your decision, use the <EditorialLink slug="idr-forgiveness-taxes">forgiveness and taxes guide</EditorialLink> to identify the tax questions to check separately.

### A concrete comparison of payment formulas

For an illustrative borrower with **$45,000 AGI**, no dependents, and loans qualifying for both plans, RAP uses the **4% band**: $45,000 × 4% ÷ 12 = **$150 per month**. Under older-borrower IBR, using the 2026 contiguous-states one-person poverty guideline **$15,960**, the base formula is ($45,000 − 150% × $15,960) × 15% ÷ 12 = **$263.25** before any applicable cap or servicing adjustments. For a borrower qualifying for newer-borrower IBR, the analogous 10% calculation is **$175.50**.

This **compares formulas, not personal eligibility**. Household size, plan-specific loan type, prior counts, spouse debt, unpaid interest and future income can all change the modeled outcome. See the [FSA plan eligibility and monthly-payment table](https://studentaid.gov/articles/save/).

## Common mistakes

- Choosing IBR from a comparison without confirming you can enroll.
- Using the same household count automatically for IBR and RAP.
- Treating the lowest initial payment as the lowest total paid.
