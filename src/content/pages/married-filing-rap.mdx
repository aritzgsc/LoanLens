## Who this applies to

Use this comparison if you are married, have loans eligible for RAP, and want to see the effect of changing which income and dependents appear on your tax return. If both spouses have debt, estimate each spouse’s payment separately.

## What to check

### Build a separate set of inputs for each return

| Input | Joint return | Separate return |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Income | Combined adjusted gross income from the joint return. | Borrower’s adjusted gross income. |
| Dependents | Dependents on the joint return. | Dependents claimed on that borrower’s return. |
| Eligible loans | Borrower’s eligible balance and spouse’s eligible balance, if used in the rule. | Borrower’s eligible balance. |

Use the [married RAP tool](/tools/married-rap-impact/) and enter each spouse’s share of joint income once. If starting from the joint total, make sure the two entries sum to that total. Avoid entering the entire joint amount in both fields. Enter separate-filing dependents independently because the count may change when the couple splits claims.

### Check the household payment

A spouse’s eligible debt can affect the share of a joint calculation assigned to each borrower. Some debts may be ineligible for the allocation. Inspect both federal loan lists before including a balance. Use the borrower’s own balance in the first run, reverse the borrower and spouse roles in the second run, and add the resulting monthly payments when both spouses are on RAP. If a spouse is on a different plan, use their actual bill or a separate comparison.

The minimum payment applies to the borrower’s result. For scenarios near that floor, a smaller calculated share might not lower the bill. Inspect the displayed amounts before treating a reduction in one borrower’s income or debt share as a savings for the household.

### Decide what the comparison includes

The repayment tool estimates student loan bills. It does not compute the joint-versus-separate tax bill, changes in credits or deductions, or how income may be reported under state rules. Compare annual loan payments with the change in annual tax cost from the same filing assumptions. A smaller bill for one spouse can coexist with higher total household costs.

For a longer projection, track the date each spouse’s plan uses updated income. A borrower’s bill can change when new tax information is used and a spouse’s balance can fall at a different pace. LoanLens holds the spouse’s eligible balance at the value entered. If that balance is about to be repaid, run a second scenario with a lower balance and compare what changes before relying on the projected total.

## Common mistakes

- Entering a joint income twice by copying it to both spouse fields.
- Using the joint dependent count for each separate return.
- Looking at one spouse’s payment and assuming it represents the whole household.
